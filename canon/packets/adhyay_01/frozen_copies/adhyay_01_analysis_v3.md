## 1. Chapter overview

Adhyay 1 opens the granth with an invocation (मंगलाचरण) to Shri Ganesha (गणपती), Pandurang (पंढरीशा), Shiva (त्र्यंबकेश्वर), Saraswati (शारदा), the kuladevata Mahalakshmi of Kolhapur (कोल्हापुरवासिनी जगन्माता), Dattatreya, the rishis, Shankaracharya and a long list of saints, and the author expresses his humility (अज्ञान मंदमती). It then introduces Shegaon (शेगांव) in Vanhad (वन्हाड) and says that Shri Gajanan appeared there in the prime of youth (ऐन तारुण्याभीतरीं) on Magh Vadya Saptami (माघ वद्य सप्तमी), within the Shaka 1800s. The core narrative is a single incident at the house of the Brahmin Devidas (देविदास विप्र): Gajanan, almost naked, sits on the road picking leftover rice grains from leaf plates, and two passers-by, Bankatlal Agarwal and Damodarpant Kulkarni, first take him for mad, then serve him a feast, then fetch water for him. The chapter ends with Gajanan's teaching that water and drinker are one, his flight "like the wind", and a promise that the next chapter will continue the story. The colophon reads "इति श्रीगजानन विजय ग्रंथस्य प्रथमोऽध्यायः समाप्तः" and the author signs off "ईश्वरासी दासगणू" with "श्रीहरिहारार्पणमस्तु".

## 2. Story list

Adhyay 1 has no separate tales. It has a frame (invocation, saint-praise, background) and one continuous incident, which I split into **5 episodes**. Count is 5 narrative episodes, plus 2 non-narrative frame sections.

### Frame (not counted as story)

- **A. Invocation and humility** - ovi 1-41. Ganesha (ovi 1-4), Saraswati (ovi 5-6), Pandurang (ovi 10-12), Ram and Krishna (ovi 13-15), Shiva (ovi 18-19), Kuladevi Mahalakshmi of Kolhapur (ovi 22), Bhavani of Tuljapur (ovi 23), Dattatreya (ovi 24), rishis and Shankaracharya (ovi 25), saints (ovi 26-29), and the author's plea for the work to be guided (ovi 29-32). Moral: the author asks the listeners to pay attention (ovi 33-38).
- **B. Saints and earlier works** - ovi 39-56. Saints as the source of grace in Bharatkhand (ovi 39-41), a long list of saints (ovi 42-50), and the author's earlier books (ovi 51-54), with a reference at ovi 56 to the saint near Akot, who is Gajanan Maharaj himself: the first great saint Dasganu met, whose biography is written last, with the reason promised.
- **C. Shegaon and the request** - ovi 57-68. Shegaon described as a small but famous village in Vanhad, with a famous lotus-pond (ovi 58-60). Ramchandra Patil asks the author to write this account during the Kartiki pilgrimage to Pandhari (ovi 65-68).
- **D. Background: Gajanan's arrival** - ovi 69-83. Gajanan is described as a "gem" and as an avatar (ovi 69-82). The date is given as Magh Vadya Saptami, year within Shaka 1800s (ovi 73-74, 83). Some people say he came from Sajjangad (ovi 75), but the author says this needs proof (ovi 76). Because nobody knew his parents or origin, the author cites the womb-less (ayonisambhava) births of the Nath yogis Matsyendranath, Gorakhnath, Changdev and Kanifnath (ovi 78-80, Kanifnath said to have appeared in an elephant's ear, गजकर्ण) and concludes that something similarly miraculous must be true of Gajanan, to whom all stages of yoga are naturally native (user-confirmed context, 2026-10-22).

### Episode 1 - The leftover leaf plates on the road

- **Ovi range:** 84-96.
- **Retelling:** Devidas, a devout householder (भाविक गृहस्थ) of the Patur lineage (पातूरकराचा वंशज), of the Madhyandin branch (शाखा माध्यंदिन), was preparing a feast for a ritual of his son (ovi 84-86). Used leaf plates (उष्ट्या पत्रावळी) had been thrown on the road in front of his house (ovi 86-87). A man (Gajanan) sat there, almost naked (दिगंबर, ovi 91; at most an old torn vest, बंडी, ovi 87-88), with no title or name on him. He held a clay pipe he had made (कच्ची चिलीम) and a gourd vessel (भोपळा) for water (ovi 89). His gaze was fixed on his nose-tip, and he shone like the young morning sun (ovi 90). He searched the plates, and whichever grain of leftover rice (शीत) fell into his sight, he picked up and put in his mouth (ovi 92-93). The Upanishad phrase "अन्नं ब्रह्मेति" is quoted (ovi 94). Bankatlal, walking past with a friend, saw this (ovi 96-97).
- **Moral/teaching:** Food is Brahman (ovi 94). Ovi 95-96 say the common man does not understand this (भावार्थ तो कळला नसे).

### Episode 2 - Two observers test the stranger

- **Ovi range:** 96-110.
- **Retelling:** Bankatlal Agarwal and his friend Damodarpant Kulkarni watch and are amazed (ovi 97-98). They debate whether the man is mad (वेडा) or a real wise man (ovi 99-100). Thousands of people passed by without noticing (ovi 105-106). They decide to test him by looking at the scraps and his conduct (ovi 101-106). Bankatlal steps forward humbly (ovi 108), asks him why he is searching plates (ovi 109), and gets no answer, only a steady look at both men (ovi 110).
- **Moral/teaching:** Discernment is needed (ovi 105, the connoisseur picks the gem from stones). Outward appearance can mislead (ovi 99, 104).

### Episode 3 - The feast at the door: mad or wise?

- **Ovi range:** 111-121.
- **Retelling:** Gajanan's face and body are described (ovi 111, see Characters). He is absorbed in inner bliss and greets them silently (ovi 112). Bankatlal asks Devidas, "lovingly", to quickly bring a plate outside (ovi 113). Devidas brings a plate full of sweet dishes (पक्वान्नांनीं भरलेले पात्र) and places it at the door before the Swami (ovi 114). Gajanan sits and eats, with no craving for any taste, leaving only a little (ovi 115). He eats the whole mixed meal (ovi 118). Bankatlal then admits he was wrong to call him mad (ovi 119), and compares Gajanan to Arjuna, who feigned madness for Subhadra in Dwarka (ovi 120-121).
- **Moral/teaching:** Admitting one's mistake (ovi 119). Renunciation of worldly "taste" (ovi 115-118). Ovi 121 says the madness here is a higher wisdom (ज्ञानजेठीं).

### Episode 4 - Midday thirst and the muddy water

- **Ovi range:** 122-137.
- **Retelling:** Dasganu uses the image of a jahagir (a large estate gifted by a king) to say that God (Hari) has given Gajanan Maharaj the estate of desirelessness (निरिच्छा, ovi 122); it is a metaphor, not a land grant. It is midday and the ground is hot; birds have gone to the trees, but Gajanan sits in the sun in joy (ovi 123-124). After eating, the gourd is found to be empty of water (ovi 125). Damodar offers to fetch water, "if you wish, this servant is ready" (ovi 126). Gajanan laughs and answers that water is needed because the body eats food, and that worldly conduct must be followed (ovi 127-131). Both men rejoice, Bankatlal calls their fortune blessed, and Damodar goes home for water (ovi 132-133). Meanwhile Gajanan goes to a well where cattle drink and drinks there, then belches with satisfaction (ovi 134). Damodar returns with a गडवा of water (ovi 135) and cries "हां हां" not to drink the muddy trough water, which is fit only for animals (ovi 136; speaker likely Damodarpant, not named in the verse); he offers sweet, cool, clear water scented with vetiver (वाळा) (ovi 137).
- **Moral/teaching:** Practical conduct (व्यवहार) still matters even for a realised soul (ovi 129-131).

### Episode 5 - Oneness of water and drinker; the disappearance

- **Ovi range:** 138-146 (ends at 145, colophon at 146).
- **Retelling:** Gajanan says "do not tell me worldly tales" (ovi 138). He says the Brahman pervades all things, that clear, muddy and scented water are the same (ovi 139-140), and that the drinker too is not separate from the water (ovi 141). God's play (ईश्वराची अगाध लीला) is known in this human birth (ovi 141). He tells them to set aside worldly attachment and contemplate, since from what did the world arise (ovi 142). Both men are moved and ready to fall at his feet (ovi 143). Knowing their intent, Gajanan runs off quickly like the wind, and no one can stop him (ovi 144). The author says the next chapter will continue, and asks listeners to pay attention (ovi 145), then signs off (ovi 146).
- **Moral/teaching:** Non-dual vision: "water is water" (पाणी तरी तोच आहे, ovi 140). Devotion and surrender (अनन्यभावें समर्थचरणीं, ovi 143). Ovi 139 (print-verified): "ब्रह्मे व्याप्त साचार", all this is pervaded by Brahman; Vyasa is not mentioned.

**Background passage (not counted as an episode):** Gajanan's arrival in Shegaon in the prime of youth on Magh Vadya Saptami (ovi 71-83). Ovi 72 says that those who see his radiance (तेज त्याचें पाहोन) are absorbed in it; the gem/diamond metaphor runs through ovi 61-72.

## 4. Places, objects, time and season

### Places
- **Shegaon (शेगांव)**, a small but famous village in Vanhad (वन्हाड, ovi 58-59). Described as "a mine of gems" (खाणीचा, ovi 61). The village has a pond or lake (सरोवर, ovi 60), where the "lotus" of Gajanan opened.
- **Vanhad (वन्हाड)**, the region (ovi 58).
- **Khamgaon taluka (खामगांव तालुक्यात)**, a nearby trading centre (ovi 58). The text says "व्यापार चाले जेथ मोठा".
- **Akot (आकोट)** area, the place where Dasganu first met the saint who is Gajanan Maharaj (ovi 56).
- **Pandhari / Pandharpur (पंढरी)**, the pilgrimage place where Ramchandra Patil made his request (ovi 65).
- **Kolhapur (कोल्हापूर)**, home of the kuladevi Mahalakshmi (ovi 22).
- **Tuljapur (तुळजा)**, Bhavani's shrine (ovi 23).
- **Shirdi (शिर्डी)**, Sai Samartha (ovi 28).
- **Dwarka (द्वारका)**, Arjuna's story (ovi 120).
- **Gokul on the Yamuna (यमुनातीरीं गोकुळांत)**, Krishna's place (ovi 14).
- **Sajjangad (सज्जनगड)**, mentioned as a possible origin of Gajanan (ovi 75). The author says this needs proof (ovi 76).
- **In Shegaon (main scene):**
  - The road in front of Devidas's house, where used leaf plates were thrown (ovi 86-87, 92, 96).
  - The door of Devidas's house, where the feast was served (ovi 114).
  - A well (कुप), where cattle drink (ovi 134).
  - Trees (वृक्ष), where birds sheltered at midday (ovi 123).
  - "जहागीरगांव" (ovi 122) is a metaphor (God gave Gajanan the estate of desirelessness), not a place.

### Objects
- **Leaf plates** (पत्रावळी, ovi 86-92): the used plates thrown on the road are the source of the scraps.
- **Plate of cooked dishes** (पक्वान्न, पात्र, ovi 114-118): a metal or leaf plate with sweet dishes, placed at the door.
- **Gourd vessel** (भोपळा, ovi 89): Gajanan's water container. Ovi 125 refers to "तुंबा" (bottle gourd), a similar item.
- **Clay pipe** (कच्ची चिलीम, ovi 89): raw clay pipe, self-made.
- **Old torn vest** (बंडी, जुन्या पुराण्या कापडाची, ovi 87-88): at most this; he is otherwise almost naked (ovi 92).
- **Earthen pot** (गडवा, ovi 135): Damodar's water container.
- **Vetiver** (वाळा, ovi 137): aromatic root added to water for scent.
- **Jaggery drink** (गुळवण, ovi 116): mentioned rhetorically.
- **Lotus** (कमल, ovi 60): metaphor for Gajanan's arrival in Shegaon.

### Time and season
- **Year:** "शके अठराशाभीतरीं" (ovi 73), within the Shaka 1800s (exact year not stated).
- **Month and day:** Magh (माघ) Vadya Saptami (वद्य सप्तमी), the 7th day of the dark fortnight (ovi 74, 83).
- **Time of day:** "दोन प्रहरच्या समयाला" (ovi 118), about the second prahar, and "सूर्य माध्यान्हीं आला" (ovi 123), the sun reached midday.
- **Weather:** "भर उन्हात" (ovi 124), full sun; "तप्त झाला भूमीचा" (ovi 123), ground heated; "गोड निर्मळ थंडगार" (ovi 137), sweet, clear and cool water.
- **Season:** the Magh month is winter in the Indian calendar (inferred). Magh Vadya Saptami = 23 Feb 1878 (late winter), but midday in Vidarbha is already scorching, and the author uses the heat to contrast with the saint sitting blissful in the open (user-supplied explanation; see Open questions).

## 5. Open questions and uncertain words

1. **"भोपळा" (ovi 89):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): gourd water-vessel, confirmed.
2. **"मठाधिपती" (ovi 85):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): head of an organisation (math).
3. **"ऋतुशांति" (ovi 86):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): confirmed as a ritual for Devidas's son.
4. **"दिगंबर" (ovi 92, print-verified) vs. "बंडी" (ovi 88):** ovi 92 says the whole form is digambar (naked) while ovi 87 describes an old vest. RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-21): digambar = naked, so consider him almost naked.
5. **"दंड गर्दन पिळदार" (ovi 111):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): "दंड" = upper arm / bicep. So: muscular upper arms and neck.
6. **"भ्रूकुटी ठायीं झाली असे" (ovi 111):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): भ्रूकुटी = the spot between the two eyebrows (also called त्रिकुटी / आज्ञाचक्र); "ठायीं झाली" = became steady, concentrated, absorbed. Meaning: his attention/gaze was fixed on the point between the eyebrows (a meditative state).
7. **"शीत पडल्या दृष्टीप्रत" (ovi 93):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): शीत = a grain of leftover cooked rice; whichever grain fell into his sight, he picked it up and put it in his mouth (ते मुखी उचलुनी घालीत). Not "cold".
8. **"वासित केलें साचार। वाळा घालून" (ovi 137):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): made fragrant by adding वाळा (vetiver / khus roots, used in India to cool and scent drinking water).
9. **"निरिच्छा हा जहागीरगांव। दिला हरीनें जयाला" (ovi 122):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): निरिच्छा = absolute desirelessness; जहागीर = a large estate gifted by a king as an unconditional reward; दिला हरीनें = given by Hari (God/Vishnu); जयाला = to whom (Gajanan Maharaj). Metaphor: God gave Gajanan Maharaj the estate of desirelessness. Corrects my earlier reading that this was about Shegaon the village.
10. **Akot saint (ovi 56):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): the saint near Akot is Gajanan Maharaj himself. Dasganu had written about many other saints first; Gajanan was the first great saint he met near Akot, yet his biography is written last, and the author says he will explain why.
11. **"सुभद्रावर" (ovi 42):** RESOLVED (user, 2026-10-22): सुभद्रा (Krishna's sister, Arjuna's wife) + वर (husband) = Arjuna.
12. **"कमाल" (ovi 45) and "कानिफा गजकर्णात" (ovi 80):** RESOLVED (user-supplied, 2026-10-22). कमाल = Saint Kamal, son and disciple of Kabir, named in the line "कबीर कमाल सूरदास". कानिफा = Kanifnath, one of the nine Navnath yogis; "गजकर्णात" literally "in an elephant's ear" (Nath lore says he did penance hidden in Airavata's ear). CONFIRMED (user, 2026-10-22): at ovi 78-80 Dasganu lists the womb-less (ayonisambhava) births of Matsyendranath, Gorakhnath, Changdev and Kanifnath to address the mystery of Gajanan's unknown origin: something similarly divine must be true of him, since all stages of yoga are native to him. Caveat: this was supplied by the user and has not been re-checked against the page images.
13. **Speaker of ovi 136-137:** RESOLVED (user-supplied, 2026-10-22): Damodarpant Kulkarni ("Pant"), who arrives with the water in a गडवा, asks Maharaj not to drink the muddy water ("fit only for animals") and offers clear, cool, sweet vetiver-scented water. Maharaj answers with the non-dual reply that follows. Note: the source quoted different verse numbers (35-37) than my ovi numbering (136-137), likely a different edition's numbering; the content matches.

## A. Scene-by-scene breakdown

### Frame, ovi 1-68 (short note)

No scene, no setting. Invocation (1-38), praise of saints and list of earlier saint-biographies (39-55), the author's reason for writing Gajanan's life last (56-57), Shegaon praised through metaphors (58-65), and Ramchandra Patil's request at Pandharpur (66-68). Visual imagery is only metaphorical: a lotus opening in "the Shegaon lake" whose fragrance fills the universe (60), Shegaon as a mine and Gajanan as its diamond (61), the Gajanan story as a great cloud and listeners as peacocks who will dance when it rains (63). These could be used as illustrative cut-aways but are not events.

### Background, ovi 69-83 (no scene)

The author reasons about Gajanan's origin (see section D and F). Only datable fact: he appeared in Shegaon in the prime of youth, Magh Vadya Saptami, within Shaka 1800s (74, 83). Ovi 83-84 tie the story that follows to "that time": "त्या वेळची तुम्हां कथा" (tyaa veLachii tumhaa kathaa, "the story of that time", 84), i.e. the incident is told as happening on the day he appeared (83: "ज्या दिवशी हा उदय पावला", jyaa divashii haa uday paavalaa).

### Scene 1 - Gajanan among the leftover leaf plates (ovi 84-96)

- **Location:** the road in front of the house of Devidas, a Brahmin of Shegaon. "उष्ट्या पत्रावळी रस्त्यावर ... घराचिया समोर" (ushTyaa patraavaLii rastyaavar ... gharaachiyaa samor, 87).
- **Time of day:** not stated in this scene. The feast is being prepared (86) and used plates have already been thrown out (87), so some guests have already eaten. Noon is reached only at ovi 118 and 123.
- **Weather and light:** not stated in this scene. The only light image is a simile for Gajanan himself: like the young sun in the east (91).
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. Devidas's house is busy: preparation of a meal for the ऋतुशांति ritual of one of his sons (86). Devidas is not shown acting yet.
  2. Gajanan is already sitting at that spot ("बैसले होते तया जागीं", baisale hote tayaa jaagii, 88).
  3. He searches through the leaf plates while sitting on the road (93).
  4. Whatever grain of rice falls into his sight, he picks up and puts in his mouth (94).
  5. Narrator explains the purpose: to make known that food is the supreme Brahman (94-96).
- **Emotion/mood:** Gajanan: calm posture (मुद्रा शांत, 91), no sense of "mine and other" (भाव मावळला आपपर, bhaav maavaLalaa aapapar, 92), acting "only as his own play" (केवळ निजलीलेनें, keval nijaliilene, 93). Narrator calls him "दयाघन" (dayaaghan, cloud of compassion, 96).
- **Strange event, literally as seen:** "तपोबल अंगी झळकत" (tapobal angii jhaLakat, 91): the power of austerity shines/glitters on his body. Nothing else supernatural is described.

### Scene 2 - Two passers-by watch and argue (ovi 97-107)

- **Location:** same road. Bankatlal "was walking along the road" (होता रस्त्यानें चालला, hotaa rastyaane chaalalaa, 97).
- **Time / weather / light:** not stated.
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. Bankatlal Agarwal, walking with his friend, sees what is happening (97).
  2. The friend is Damodarpant Kulkarni; both are astonished (आश्चर्यचकित, 98).
  3. They talk to each other: his actions are odd, he looks like a madman (99).
  4. Their reasoning: if he were after food he would ask for a plate, and Devidas would give it, since Devidas is a good man (100); a wise host never turns away a beggar at the door, so no reasoning fits his actions (101).
  5. Bankatlal to Pant: let us just stand here on the road to test his actions (102).
  6. Speaker continues (most likely Bankatlal, see G): Vyasa says in the Bhagavat that true sadhus outwardly behave like madmen (103); by his actions he looks mad, but he may be a cartload of knowledge (104).
  7. Narrator: only a jeweller knows a gem (105); thousands of people went along the road, but no one except these two saw him (106); diamonds and pebbles lie mixed, the jeweller picks the diamond (107).
- **Emotion/mood:** both men: astonishment (98), puzzlement and debate (99-104), curiosity strong enough to stop and wait (102).
- **Strange event:** the crowd's blindness: "पथे हजारो लोक गेले। परी न कोणी पाहिले" (pathe hajaaro lok gele, parii na koNii paahile, 106). Literally: many people pass by on the road and nobody looks at him.

### Scene 3 - The question, the silence, the bow (ovi 108-113)

- **Location:** same spot. Bankatlal steps forward (108); Gajanan is still seated.
- **Time / weather / light:** not stated.
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. Bankatlal steps forward first, humbly (108).
  2. He asks: "I don't understand why you are searching these leaf plates. If you are hungry, we will arrange food." (109)
  3. No answer. Gajanan only looks up at the faces of both men (नुसतें वरी पाहिलें। उभयतांच्या मुखाकडे, nusate varii paahile, ubhayataanchyaa mukhaakaDe, 110).
  4. Close description of Gajanan (111): radiant, muscular arms and neck, broad chest, steady gaze, attention fixed between the eyebrows.
  5. "Such a yogi, absorbed in inner bliss, was seen; a namaskar was made in silence, the heart content" (112). In context the two men are the ones who see and bow (see F2).
  6. They (plural verb "सांगू लागले", saangu laagale) tell "Devidasbuva" lovingly: quickly serve a plate and bring it outside (113).
- **Emotion/mood:** Bankatlal: humility (108), concern (109). Gajanan: silent, inward bliss (112). The men: contentment (चित्तीं संतोष, 112), affection (प्रेमेसीं, 113), urgency (वेगेंसी, 113).

### Scene 4 - The plate at the door (ovi 114-122)

- **Location:** in front of Devidas's door: "द्वारासमोर स्वामींपुढे" (dvaaraasamor svaamiinpuDhe, 114).
- **Time of day:** the meal satisfies his hunger "at the time of two prahar" (दोन प्रहरच्या समयाला, 118), i.e. around noon.
- **Weather / light:** not stated in this scene.
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. Devidas does as asked: brings a plate filled with पक्वान्नें (rich cooked dishes) and sets it before the Swami, in front of the door (114).
  2. Gajanan sits down at the plate to eat (115).
  3. No craving for any taste remains in him, not even an atom (115).
  4. Narrator: one who has drunk the nectar of Brahman would not ask for jaggery-water and sit smacking his lips (116); an emperor does not care for a small estate (117).
  5. He mixes all the dishes into one and eats; likes and dislikes are gone; he satisfies the fire of hunger (118).
  6. Bankatlal, seeing this, says to Pant: calling him mad was certainly a mistake (119).
  7. He compares: Arjuna became mad like this at Dwarka for Subhadra, forgot worldly conduct and did odd things (120); this one, eldest in knowledge, has gone "mad" for Subhadra-as-liberation; no need to test him any more (121).
  8. "Blessed is our Shegaon; we have seen the king of yogis with our own eyes, to whom Hari gave desirelessness as his estate" (122; speaker probably still Bankatlal, see B and G).
- **Emotion/mood:** Gajanan: indifferent to taste, eats fully (115, 118). Bankatlal: self-correction, reverence, local pride ("धन्य आपुले शेगांव", 122). Devidas: compliant, no words.

### Scene 5 - Noon heat and the empty gourd (ovi 123-133)

- **Location:** same spot, in the open.
- **Time of day:** midday: "सूर्य माध्यान्हीं आला" (suurya maadhyaanhii aalaa, 123).
- **Weather and light:** the ground is heated (भाग भूमीचा तप्त झाला, 123); birds have gone to sit in the trees for shelter (पांखरेही आश्रयाला जाऊन बैसलीं वृक्षावरी, 123); full sun (भर उन्हात, 124).
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. Gajanan sits in the full sun, joyful; he is Brahman itself; no fear of anything remains in him (124).
  2. Bankatlal (addressing "पंता") notes: he has eaten to his fill, there is no water in his gourd (तुंबा); Pant, let us bring water for him (125).
  3. Damodar asks Gajanan: there is no water in the gourd; if you wish, this servant is ready to bring water (126).
  4. Gajanan laughs, looks at both, and speaks (127): if you need it, bring water; one Brahman fills the whole world (128); between you and us no difference remains, yet worldly conduct must be followed (129); the bodies ate food, so they need water; clever people must know this conduct (130); so, for your cleverness, if you really need it, arrange the water, and that is all (131).
  5. Both rejoice; Bankatlal to Pant: our fortune is blessed (132).
  6. Damodar goes into a house ("घरांत गेले", gharaat gele) to fetch water (133). Whose house is not stated (see F8).
- **Emotion/mood:** Gajanan: joy, fearlessness (124), amusement (laughs, 127), a gently ironic tone toward their "cleverness" (131). Damodar: eager service, self-humbling ("हा चाकर", haa chaakar, this servant, 126). Both men: joy (हर्षले, harshale, 132).

### Scene 6 - Gajanan drinks at the cattle trough; Damodar returns (ovi 133-137)

- **Location:** beside a well there is a हाळ (haaL, probably a cattle water trough; see G) where all the animals drink (134). Distance from Devidas's door: not stated. Gajanan goes there (तेथें जाऊन, tethe jaaun, 135).
- **Time / weather:** same midday; nothing new stated.
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. While Damodar is away ("मग इकडे काय घडला तो ऐका", the narrator turns to "here", 133).
  2. Gajanan goes to the trough by the well, drinks the water there (135).
  3. He belches with satisfaction (तृप्ततेचे ढेकर दिले, truptateche Dhekar dile, 135).
  4. Just then Pant arrives with water in a गडवा (135).
  5. Pant: "Oh, oh! Samarth, do not put that muddy water to your mouth; it is fit for animals to drink" (136).
  6. "See, I have brought water: sweet, clear, cool, made fragrant with vetiver" (137).
- **Emotion/mood:** Gajanan: contentment (belch, 135). Damodar: alarm and concern ("हां हां", haa haa, 136), then eager offering (137).
- **Literal visual:** the muddy water is the trough water Gajanan has just drunk, not the water Damodar brings (see F1).

### Scene 7 - The teaching and the run (ovi 138-144)

- **Location:** same place by the trough/well (no move is stated).
- **Time / weather:** not restated.
- **Characters and beats:**
  1. Maharaj speaks: do not tell us these worldly tales (138).
  2. All moving and unmoving things are pervaded by Brahman; there the difference of muddy, clear and scented water does not remain (139).
  3. Water is the same, clear or muddy; good smell and bad smell are both its forms (140).
  4. The drinker too is not apart from it; God's play is unfathomable, and it can be known in this human birth (141).
  5. "You have dropped that; your mind is entangled in worldly dealings; always reflect on this: from what did the world come?" (142).
  6. Both men are overcome with emotion (गेले गहिवरोनि, gele gahivaroni) and are ready to roll at his feet in total devotion (लोळावया तयार झाले, loLaavayaa tayaar jhaale, 143).
  7. Knowing their intent, Maharaj leaves, running and running (पळत पळत), at the speed of the wind; "who in the world can stop him?" (144).
- **Emotion/mood:** Gajanan: firm, teaching; avoids being worshipped (leaves on knowing their intent, 144). Both men: choked with emotion, surrender (143), presumably left behind (not stated how they react to his departure).
- **Strange event, literally as seen:** he runs off "with the speed of the wind" (वायूच्या त्या गतिप्रत, vaayuuchyaa tyaa gatiprat, 144). The text does not say he vanished; it says he ran fast and no one could hold him back.

### Close (ovi 145-146)

Narrator: the next story comes in chapter 2 (145); the author, hands folded, prays that the book gladden devotees (146); colophon.

## C. Visual details stated in the text

**Per scene**

- **Scene 1 (84-96):** used (उष्ट्या, ushTyaa) leaf plates lying on the road in front of a Brahmin's house (87). Gajanan seated on the road among them (88, 93). On his body one बंडी of old worn cloth (88), yet "the whole form is digambar" (92). Near him no possession or trappings at all ("कोणत्याही उपाधीचें नांव नव्हतें जवळीं", 89) except: one gourd as his water vessel (89), and in his hand an unfired clay chilim he made himself, which "had never seen a potter's kiln" ("कुंभाराच्या भट्टीप्रत जिनें नव्हतें पाहिलें", kumbhaaraachyaa bhaTTiiprat jine navhate paahile, 90). Gaze on the tip of the nose, calm posture, body glittering with ascetic power, like the young sun in the east (91). Action: searches the plates, picks single rice grains he sees and puts them in his mouth (93-94).
- **Scene 2 (97-107):** two men walking on the road, then standing on the road to watch (102). Crowd: "thousands" pass on the road without looking (106). Colors, clothing of the two men: not stated.
- **Scene 3 (108-113):** Bankatlal steps forward ahead of Damodar (108). Gajanan, seated, looks up ("वरी", varii) at both faces (110). Body (111): radiant complexion, muscular upper arms and neck, broad chest, steady gaze, attention between the eyebrows. Silent bow (112, by the men in my reading). Request to Devidas to bring a plate "outside" (बाहेर, baaher, 113), so the house interior and the road are separate spaces.
- **Scene 4 (114-122):** a plate (पात्र; material not stated) full of पक्वान्नें, placed in front of the door, before Gajanan (114). He sits at it to eat (115). He mixes all the dishes together into one (अवघीं पक्वान्नें एक केलीं, avaghii pakvaanne ek kelii, 118). He does not smack his lips (implied by the contrast at 116). Eats till hunger is satisfied (118).
- **Scene 5 (123-133):** noon sun, hot ground, birds sitting in trees (123); Gajanan sitting in the full sun (124). His gourd is now called तुंबा (tumbaa, 125-126) and is empty. Sound: Gajanan laughs (हास्य केलें, 127), then speaks looking at both (127). Damodar walks off into a house (133).
- **Scene 6 (133-137):** a well (कूप) with a हाळ beside it, where all the animals drink (134). Gajanan goes there, drinks, and belches audibly (135). Damodar arrives with a गडवा of water (135; material not stated). Two waters on screen: the trough water, called muddy (गढूळ, 136), and Damodar's water: sweet, clear, cool, vetiver-scented (137). Sound: Damodar's "हां हां" (136).
- **Scene 7 (138-144):** speech only, then both men emotional and about to prostrate/roll at his feet (143). Exit: Gajanan sets off running, "पळत पळत", at wind speed (144). Where he runs to: not stated.

**Across the chapter**

- Colors: none named. The only color-like image is "प्राचीच्या बालरवीवत्" (praachiichyaa baalaraviivat, like the young sun in the east, 91), a radiance simile.
- Textures: old worn cloth (88), unfired raw clay (90), used leaf plates (87), muddy vs clear water (136-140), mixed food (118).
- Smells: vetiver fragrance (137); "सुवास कुवास" (suvaas kuvaas, good and bad smell, 140) in Gajanan's teaching.
- Sounds: laughter (127), belch (135), "हां हां" (136). Silence: no reply (110), silent namaskar (112).
- Crowd: "हजारो लोक" on the road (106); "जनावरें सारीं" at the trough (134); birds (पांखरें, 123).
- Distances: none stated (house to well, well to door, direction of his run).
- How Gajanan moves and sits: already sitting when the story starts (88); sits on the road (93); sits down to eat (115); sits in the sun (124); walks/goes to the trough (135); runs off fast (144). No standing posture is described.
- How he eats: grain by grain from the plates (94); later the full plate, all dishes mixed into one, no taste-preference (115, 118); drinks cattle-trough water (135).

## D. Continuity

**What each character carries**

- **Gajanan:** prime of youth (74); unknown origin, caste and whereabouts, which the author says do not matter (70-73); possessions limited to gourd and self-made unfired chilim (89-90), one old bandi (88), otherwise digambar (92). Knows the men's intent without being told (144: "त्यांचा जाणोन हेत", tyaanchaa jaaNon het).
- **Devidas:** a Brahmin householder of Patur lineage, Madhyandin branch, head of a math (84-85); the feast is for his son's ritual (86). Addressed as "देविदासबुवा" (113). He is willing to give food to a beggar, according to the two men (100). He is in the house; he comes out only to bring the plate (114). He does not appear again after 114.
- **Bankatlal:** walking with his friend (97); knows the Bhagavat saying (103, if he is the speaker) and the Arjuna-Subhadra story (120); proud of "our Shegaon" (122), which suggests he is a Shegaon resident (inference from "आपुले").
- **Damodarpant:** Bankatlal's friend (97, 98); has access to a house nearby where he gets water and vetiver (133, 137); thinks muddy trough water is for animals (136).

**What the observers know, and how it changes**

1. Before: they do not know who he is; nobody else notices him (106).
2. First judgment: mad (99).
3. Doubt: maybe a "cartload of knowledge" (104); they decide to test (102).
4. After the meal: Bankatlal declares the "mad" verdict a mistake and says no more testing is needed (119-121); he calls him "योगीराव" (122).
5. After the water teaching: both are moved and ready to surrender at his feet (143).
6. End: he runs off; they have no name, no address and no way to find him (not stated how they react; ovi 145 defers to chapter 2).

**What changes in Gajanan's situation:** from unfed and unseen (scene 1) to served at the door (114), then to being asked (126), then he leaves (144). His own state does not change (silent bliss, 112; joy, 124; laughter, 127).

**Time continuity:** the whole incident runs in one stretch from before noon (plates already thrown) to noon (118, 123), on Magh Vadya Saptami of the year of his appearance (83-84).

**Object continuity:** the water vessel is "भोपळा" at 89 and "तुंबा" at 125-126 (same object in context). Plates: thrown leaf plates (87) → Devidas's full plate (114). Water: empty gourd (125) → trough water drunk (135) → Damodar's vetiver water in a गडवा (135-137), which Gajanan is not said to drink.

## E. Character-sheet gaps (new items only)

**Gajanan**
- "आधुनिक संत चूडामणी" (aadhunik sant chuuDaamaNii, crest-jewel of modern saints, 69); "महापुरुष" (69).
- Caste, whereabouts and family are not known, and the author says a historical view need not ask (ठावठिकाण ... जातीचा, 70).
- "ज्ञानराशी" (jnyaanaraashii, heap of knowledge, 83); "ज्ञानजेठी" (jnyaanajeThii, eldest in knowledge, 121).
- "भाव मावळला आपपर" (bhaav maavaLalaa aapapar, sense of self/other has set, 92).
- "केवळ निजलीलेनें" (keval nijaliilene, only as his own play, 93).
- "दयाघन" (dayaaghan, cloud of compassion, 96).
- Chilim is unfired, self-made: "कुंभाराच्या भट्टीप्रत जिनें नव्हतें पाहिलें" (90).
- No trappings at all near him: "कोणत्याही उपाधीचें नांव नव्हतें जवळीं" (89).
- Looks up silently instead of answering (110).
- Mixes all dishes together into one before eating (118); does not smack lips (116).
- Fearless: "भय ना कशाचें उरलें या" (bhay naa kashaache urale yaa, 124).
- Laughs before speaking (127) and has a mildly ironic manner: "तुमच्या चातुर्यासी" (tumachyaa chaaturyaasii, for your cleverness, 131).
- Speaks of himself in the plural "आम्ही / आम्हां" (129, 138).
- Drinks from the animals' trough; belches with satisfaction (135).
- Avoids being worshipped: leaves on knowing they want to fall at his feet (144).
- Moves fast: "वायूच्या त्या गतिप्रत" (144).
- Voice pitch, height, hair, beard: still not stated.

**Devidas**
- Title "बुवा": "देविदासबुवासी" (devidaasabuvaasii, 113).
- Called "सज्जन" again by the observers (100), who also say he would feed any beggar who asked (100-101).
- Acts quickly and without speech (114).

**Bankatlal**
- Shown walking along the road when the story begins (97).
- Offers food before he knows who Gajanan is (109).
- Uses Puranic examples: Arjuna and Subhadra (120-121), and likely Vyasa's Bhagavat (103).
- Local pride: "धन्य आपुले शेगांव" (122, if he is the speaker).
- Proposes the water (125), then leaves the offer to Damodar.
- Polite form of address: "कां हो करितां" (kaa ho karitaa, 109).

**Damodarpant**
- Calls himself "हा चाकर" (this servant, 126).
- Exclamation "हां हां" (136); protective about purity of water; prepares water with care (cool, vetiver, 137).
- Is "Pant" to Bankatlal throughout (102, 119, 125, 132) and "Damodar" to the narrator (126, 133).
- Overcome with emotion at the end (गहिवरोनि, 143), together with Bankatlal.

**Dasganu (author)**
- Calls himself "लेकरूं आहे अजाण" (lekaruu aahe ajaaN, an ignorant child, 7), "तुमचें तान्हें" (tumache taanhe, your infant, 29), and a pen in the saints' hands (लेखणी, 31-32).
- Saw Gajanan himself: "जो प्रथमताच मी पाहिला। आकोटासन्निध" (jo prathamataach mii paahilaa, aakoTaasannidh, 56).
- Had long wished to write Gajanan's life but it had not come together (67); says Gajanan arranged Ramchandra Patil's request to fulfil that wish (68: "केली रामचंद्राची योजना या समर्थें").
- Explains why Gajanan comes last: a garland is strung first and the meru bead is attached at the end (57: "माळा आधीं ओविती। मग मेरुमणी जोडिती", maaLaa aadhii ovitii, mag merumaNii joDitii).
- Signs off with folded hands (हेच विनवी जोडोन हात, 146).
- Addresses listeners as "विबुध हो" (vibudh ho, 35, 54) and "श्रोते" (33, 63).

**Ramchandra Patil**
- The request was made at Pandharpur, at the Kartiki vari (66). Nothing else (no age, appearance, home) is stated.

**Author's sources (what he says he used)**
- Mahipati's saint-biographies: "ज्यांचीं चरित्रें-गायन। केलीं मागें महिपतींनी" (50); names not repeated in full; read "भक्तिविजय, भक्तमाला" (51).
- "नवनाथ भक्तिसार" as the book of the Naths' lilas (47).
- His own three earlier books on later saints, summarised there (52, 54).
- The Upanishads / Shruti for "अन्नम् ब्रह्मेति" (95).
- Vyasa's Bhagavat for "true saints behave like madmen" (103).
- Hearsay on Gajanan's origin: "कोणी कोणी म्हणती जन" (75), judged to lack strong proof (76).
- Nothing in Adhyay 1 says the Devidas incident came from a named eyewitness.

**How other people describe Gajanan (in this adhyay)**
- Some people: came from Sajjangad, the seat of "श्रीसमर्थ" (75).
- The two observers: "वेड्यापरी" (like a madman, 99), "ज्ञानगाडा" (cartload of knowledge, 104), later "योगीराव" (122).
- The crowd: does not notice him at all (106).

## F. Possible corrections to the master file

1. **Muddy water belongs to the trough, not to Damodar.** Master, Episode 4: "Damodar returns with water in an earthen pot (गडवा), which is muddy (गढूळ)." Master, Damodarpant: "first muddy, then clear, cool, and scented with vetiver". My reading: Gajanan drinks the trough water by the well (135); Damodar arrives with a गडवा of clean water (135) and tells him not to drink "that muddy water", which is fit for animals (136), and offers his own clean vetiver water (137). Damodar's water is never muddy.
2. **Who bowed silently (112).** Master: "He is absorbed in inner bliss and greets them silently (ovi 112)" and "greets silently ('मौनेंच नमस्कार केला')". My reading: "निजानंदी रंगलेला। ऐसा योगी पाहिला। मौनेंच नमस्कार केला। चित्तीं संतोष पावोनियां" most naturally has the two men as the ones who saw the yogi and bowed silently, then "सांगू लागले" (113, plural) to Devidas. Listed in G for the user to decide.
3. **"leaving only a little" (115).** Master: "Gajanan sits and eats, with no craving for any taste, leaving only a little (ovi 115)." Text: "चवी न कशाची अंतरी। अणुमात्र उरली असे" = not even an atom of taste-craving remained in him. Nothing is said about leftovers.
4. **Matsyendranath is not in ovi 80.** Master, Background D: "the womb-less births of the Nath yogis Matsyendranath, Gorakhnath, Changdev and Kanifnath". Text (80): "गोरख जन्मला उकिरड्यांत। कानिफा गजकर्णांत। चांगदेव नारायण डोहांत। योनीवांचून प्रगटले" = Gorakh born in a refuse heap (उकिरडा), Kanifa in an elephant's ear, Changdev and Narayan in a pool (डोह). (Master section 3.4 lists these names correctly.)
5. **Ovi 75-79 offer more than "needs proof".** Master: "Some people say he came from Sajjangad (ovi 75), but the author says this needs proof (ovi 76)." Text: 76 says there is no strong proof, "परी कांही तरी असावा। अर्थ त्यांच्या म्हणण्यांत" (but there must be some meaning in what they say); 77-78: the people had become fallen and troubled, and "it seems the Samarth did this marvel": he came to earth again, taking avatar in Gajanan's form (गजाननरूपें अवतार धरून आले महीवर पुन्हा); 79: a yogi can enter any body, as the Jagadguru did. The ayonija examples (80) are a further line of reasoning, and 81 concludes "something like this must have happened". Whether "समर्थ" in 77-78 means Samarth Ramdas is in G.
6. **No Vyasa at ovi 139.** Master, Episode 5: "Ovi 139 cites the sage Vyas as saying the same (ब्रह्मे व्यास साचार)" and 3.4 "Ovi 139 cites 'ब्रह्मे व्यास'". Text: "ब्रह्मे व्याप्त साचार" = truly pervaded by Brahman. Vyasa is cited at ovi 103 instead ("ऐसी व्यासाची वैखरी। बोलली आहे भागवतात").
7. **Chilim not from a potter.** Master: "described as his own making (स्वकृत), from a potter (कुंभाराच्या)". Text (90): "कुंभाराच्या भट्टीप्रत जिनें नव्हतें पाहिलें" = it had never seen a potter's kiln, i.e. unfired, self-made.
8. **Damodar's "home".** Master: "Damodar goes home for water" and "his home is where he fetches water (ovi 133, 'घरांत गेले')". Text says only "घरांत गेले" (went into the house); whose house is not stated (could be Devidas's, at whose door they stand). See G.
9. **Well vs trough.** Master: "Gajanan goes to a well where cattle drink and drinks there". Text (134): "कुपाचिया शेजारीं हाळ होता" = beside the well there was a हाळ where all the animals drank; he drank "there" (तेथें, 135), i.e. at the हाळ.
10. **"गडवा" as "earthen pot".** Master calls it an earthen pot; the material is not stated in the text.
11. **"no title or name on him" (89).** Text: "कोणत्याही उपाधीचें नांव नव्हतें जवळीं" = near him there was not even the name of any upadhi (encumbrance/possession/attribute), followed directly by "except" the gourd and chilim. "No possessions at all" fits the context better than "no title or name". See G.
12. **The reason Gajanan is written last is given, not only promised (56-57).** Master: "whose biography is written last, with the reason promised." Ovi 57 gives it: a garland is strung first, the meru bead added last.
13. **Ramchandra's request was arranged by Gajanan (68), and the author already wished to write it (67).** Master C does not mention this.
14. **Who suggests water (125).** Master: "After eating, the gourd is found to be empty of water (ovi 125)." Text: "तें पंता यालागुनी। आपण देऊं आणून" = someone says to Pant "let us bring it for him", so the speaker is Bankatlal (only he calls Damodar "Pant"), and Damodar then makes the offer (126).
15. **Ovi 109 includes a food offer.** Master: "asks him why he is searching plates (ovi 109)". Text adds "क्षुधा असेल आपणां। तरी तरतूद करूं अन्नाची" (if you are hungry, we'll arrange food).
16. **Ovi 142 is a reproach, not an instruction to set aside.** Master: "He tells them to set aside worldly attachment and contemplate". Text: "तें दिलें टाकून। व्यवहारीं गोविलें मन" = you have given that up and your mind is entangled in worldly dealings; then "always reflect on this".
17. **Ovi 121, "ज्ञानजेठीं".** Master: "Ovi 121 says the madness here is a higher wisdom (ज्ञानजेठीं)". Text: "तैसाच हा ज्ञानजेठीं। मुक्तिरूप सुभद्रेसाठीं। वेडा झाला" = likewise this one, eldest in knowledge, went mad for Subhadra in the form of liberation; "ज्ञानजेठी" describes Gajanan, not the madness.
18. **Ovi 72 is a diamond simile.** Master: "Ovi 72 says that those who see his radiance (तेज त्याचें पाहोन) are absorbed in it". Text: "जो कां हिरा तेजमान ... तेज त्याचें पाहोन ज्ञाते तल्लीन होती" = seeing the brilliance of a shining diamond, knowers are absorbed; applied to Gajanan by simile.
19. **"दोन प्रहर" (118).** Master: "about the second prahar". In Marathi usage "दोन प्रहर" is usually midday (noon), which also matches 123. Minor.
20. **Ovi 122 speaker.** Master treats it as Dasganu's image. In the text it follows Bankatlal's speech (119-121) and says "धन्य आपुले शेगांव। दृष्टी पाहिला योगीराव" ("our Shegaon", "we saw with our eyes"), so it is probably Bankatlal speaking. The meaning confirmed by the user is unchanged; only the speaker is in doubt (G).
21. **Ovi 113 speaker.** Master says Bankatlal asks Devidas; the verb is plural ("सांगू लागले") and no name is given. Could be both men or honorific Bankatlal.
22. **Shegaon "lotus-pond" (58-60).** Master: "with a famous lotus-pond (ovi 58-60)" and Places: "The village has a pond or lake (सरोवर, ovi 60)". Text: "त्या शेगांव सरोवरीं भले। गजानन कमल उदया आलें" = in that lake, Shegaon, the lotus Gajanan bloomed. Shegaon itself is the metaphorical lake; no real pond is described.
23. **Khamgaon (58).** Master: "Khamgaon taluka, a nearby trading centre". Text: "खामगांव नामें तालुक्यात। व्यापार चाले जेथ मोठा" = Shegaon is in the taluka named Khamgaon, where (Shegaon or the taluka; "जेथ" is ambiguous) big trade goes on.
24. **Ovi-number slips (one off).** In the printed numbering: "ऐन तारुण्याभीतरीं" and "शके अठराशाभीतरीं" are ovi **74** (master: 73); Ramchandra Patil's request is ovi **66** (master: 65); the chilim is ovi **90** (master: 89, the gourd is 89); "नासाग्र दृष्टि", "तपोबल", "बालरवीवत्" are ovi **91** (master: 90); "मूर्ति अवघी दिगंबर" is ovi **92** (master: 91); searching plates "बैसोनिया रस्त्यावरी" is **93** (master: 92); "शीत ... मुखी उचलुनी घालीत" is **94** (master: 93); "अन्नम् ब्रह्मेति" is **95** (master: 94); "पौरवासी" is **64** (master: 63); Gajanan drinks and belches at **135** (master: 134).

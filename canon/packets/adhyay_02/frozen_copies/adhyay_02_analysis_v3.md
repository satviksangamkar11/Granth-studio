## 1. Chapter overview

Adhyay 2 opens with a short prayer to Pandurang of Chandrabhaga (ovi 1-4), then continues straight from Adhyay 1: Gajanan Maharaj has left, and Bankatlal is restless, cannot enjoy food or water, and cannot shake Gajanan's form from his sight (ovi 5-10). His father Bhavaniram notices (ovi 11-14); the old neighbour Ramajipant Deshmukh hears the story and says the man must be a yogi (ovi 17-20). After four days (ovi 21) a travelling kirtankar, Govindbuva Takalikar, gives kirtan at the Shankar temple. Bankatlal meets the tailor Pitambar there and the two see Gajanan sitting at the back (ovi 22-29). Gajanan asks for jhunka-bhakri from the Malin (a gardener woman), makes a nearly dry, dirty stream give a gourd of clear water (ovi 31-44), asks for a betel nut and refuses money (ovi 45-52), corrects Govindbuva's kirtan from where he sits and refuses to move until Govindbuva begs him, then rebukes him (ovi 53-67). Govindbuva tells the crowd that a priceless jewel has come to Shegaon (ovi 68-71).

Bankatlal's father agrees to bring him home (ovi 72-75). Bankatlal meets Gajanan in Manik Chowk at sunset, cows gathering round him, and brings him home (ovi 76-81). On a pradosh evening the father offers bilva leaves and a tray of noon puris (ovi 82-93); Gajanan eats a great quantity and stays the night (ovi 94). The next day, a Monday, he is bathed with great ceremony and dressed in a pitambar (ovi 95-103). Bankatlal's cousin Ichcharam Shetji, a Shiva devotee on a Monday fast, worships him and offers a huge naivedya; Gajanan, speaking to himself, eats all of it and then vomits (ovi 104-128), which Dasganu compares with Ramdas Swami (ovi 122-124) and turns into a lesson against insisting. Bhajan follows; Gajanan mutters "gan gan ganat bote" and people give him the name Gajanan (ovi 129-134). The chapter ends with descriptions of the crowds at Bankatlal's house (ovi 135-142) and of Gajanan's unfixed daily routine (ovi 143-146), a promise to continue (ovi 147), and the sign-off "दासगणू ईशातें" (ovi 148).

## 2. Story list

Adhyay 2 has **10 narrative episodes**, plus 1 short embedded comparison anecdote (Ramdas and the kheer, not counted), **2 frame sections** and **2 descriptive passages** (not counted as stories). The day count between episodes is partly unclear (section 5, question 14).

### Frame sections

- **A. Prayer to Pandurang** - ovi 1-4. "जयजय अज अजिता सर्वेश्वरा / हे चंद्रभागातटविहारा" (ovi 1). Retelling: without the Lord everything is weariness (शीण) and a body without life is a corpse, "who asks after a corpse" (ovi 2); images of a lake whose beauty is its water, and of a fruit whose juicy inside gives the husk importance (ovi 3, hard, see section 5, q1); the author asks the Lord to make those who take refuge "समर्थ" and to remove sin, heat/suffering (ताप) and poverty (दैन्य) (ovi 4). Teaching: dependence on God's grace.
- **B. Closing** - ovi 147-148. "Now, for the next adhyay keep your devotion and listen; don't delay" (ovi 147). The author folds his hands and asks that this Gajanan charitra be an "आदर्श" for devotees (ovi 148), signs "दासगणू" and gives the colophon.

### Descriptive passages

- **D1. The crowds at Bankatlal's house** - ovi 135-142. Gajanan is immersed in "अस्ति-भाति-प्रिया" and the joy cannot be described (ovi 135). The crowd is compared with Ashadhi at Pandharpur, Simhastha at the Godavari bank, the Kumbh Mela at Haridwar (ovi 136). Countless people come in lines to Bankatlal's house in Shegaon (ovi 137); Gajanan is called Vitthal-Narayan; the house a temple (ovi 138-139). For one who has reached Brahmapad, caste is irrelevant, as the sun's light is alike for all (ovi 140). New yatra daily and samaradhana feasts; even Shesha would tire describing it (ovi 141). The author says he is only a worm, speaking because Gajanan makes him the cause for his mouth (ovi 142).
- **D2. Gajanan's daily routine** - ovi 143-146. No fixed rule: sometimes mangal-snan, sometimes goes into the "हाळ", sometimes drinks muddy water (ovi 144); his routine is like the motion of wind and cannot be fixed (ovi 145); he seems to love the chilim greatly but has no attachment to it, "केवळ कौतुक" (ovi 146).

### Episode 1 - Bankatlal's longing, his father, and Ramajipant

- **Ovi range:** 5-21.
- **Retelling:** In the last adhyay Samarth left, and Bankatlal began to feel "हुरहूर" (ovi 5). Food and water lost their taste; Samarth's meditation was in his mind and Gajanan's form never moved from his sight (ovi 6); wherever he looked he saw it (ovi 7). Dasganu compares him to a cow who has lost her calf (ovi 8). He could not tell this to his father (ovi 9), so he searched all Shegaon without finding a trace (ovi 10). His father Bhavaniram asked why his mood had become restless, why there was no cheer and his face was dull (ovi 11-12), "you are a young man, you lack nothing", is it a worry or a bodily illness? a son never hides a thing from his father (ovi 13-14). Bankatlal said something to satisfy his father and went off again to search Shegaon (ovi 15). His neighbour Ramajipant Deshmukh, a virtuous old man (ovi 16-17), heard his full account and said the man described must be some yogi; such conduct is not seen in an unfit person, and such darshan does not come without earlier merit (ovi 18-19). He said: your birth is blessed, may I too have darshan (ovi 20). Four days passed in this state; Bankatlal could not forget Gajanan for a moment (ovi 21).
- **Moral/teaching:** Longing for a saint after a single sight; recognising a saint needs merit from earlier births (ovi 19); an elder's discernment.

### Episode 2 - Govindbuva's kirtan; Samarth is seen; the Malin's bhakri

- **Ovi range:** 22-32.
- **Retelling:** Govindbuva Takalikar, a kirtankar, famed in Vanhad, came wandering to give kirtan at Shegaon (ovi 22-23). It was arranged in the Shankar temple and men and women ran to hear it (ovi 24). Bankatlal came too and met the tailor Pitambar, a simple devout man, to whom he told the news of Samarth (ovi 25-26). While the two sat at the kirtan they suddenly saw Samarth sitting behind them, on a "फरसावरी" (ovi 27). They left the kirtan and ran to him like a miser to a hoard of wealth (ovi 28); similes of the chatak and Swati rain, the peacock and the cloud, the chakor and the moon (ovi 29). They stood at a distance and humbly asked "shall we bring something to eat?" (ovi 30). Maharaj said: if you need to, bring jhunka-bhakri from the Malin's house (ovi 31). Bankatlal quickly brought "चून" (footnoted पिठले) and half a bhakri and placed it in the yogeshwar's hands (ovi 32).
- **Moral/teaching:** love for a saint outranks a kirtan; simple food from a humble woman is accepted.

### Episode 3 - The stream and the gourd

- **Ovi range:** 33-44.
- **Retelling:** While eating, Samarth told Pitambar to go to the stream (ओढा) and bring the gourd (तुंबा) full of water (ovi 33). Pitambar objected: the stream has very little water, no room to dip a gourd (ovi 34), and the water is spoiled by cattle and passers-by, unfit to drink (ovi 35); he offered to bring water from someone else (ovi 36). Gajanan said "दुसरें पाणी आम्हां नको" and told him to bring water from the stream itself, not by filling the gourd with cupped handfuls (ovi 36-37). Pitambar went at once and saw no water that could fill a gourd; just enough to wet the soles of the feet, and cupped hands would not fill it (ovi 38-39). Troubled, he finally touched the gourd to the water in his heart-felt faith (ovi 40). Then the unnatural happened: wherever the gourd was placed it sank and a hollow formed in the stream (ovi 41, section 5 q10). The dirty water of the stream looked like crystal in the gourd (ovi 42). The tailor was astonished: "this is truly the power of the yogeshwar; hold no doubt" (ovi 43). He brought the gourd and placed it before Yogeshwar, who accepted it after eating the jhunka-bhakri (ovi 44).
- **Moral/teaching:** faith over calculation; Pitambar's objections (ovi 34-35) are overturned when the guru's command is obeyed.

### Episode 4 - The betel nut and the coin

- **Ovi range:** 45-52.
- **Retelling:** Gajanan asked Bankatlal for supari: "अरे माळीणीच्या भाकरीवरी माझी सेवा करतोस कां?" (ovi 45), then "take the supari from your pocket and break it and give it to me" (ovi 46); Bankatlal was very pleased. Along with the supari he placed two paise, copper coins (ovi 47-48; coin words in section 5 q12). Seeing money, Gajanan said laughing: "Taking me for a trader, do you offer this to me?" (ovi 49). These coins are for your dealings, "I have no need of them; I am content with the coin of devotion" (ovi 50). Then: "that was with you; therefore I met you again; think about this and you will understand" (ovi 51, referent unclear, section 5 q13). He told both to go hear the kirtan while he would sit by the neem tree and hear it (ovi 52).
- **Moral/teaching:** money is for traders; devotion is the only coin a saint accepts.

### Episode 5 - Samarth corrects Govindbuva's kirtan

- **Ovi range:** 53-71.
- **Retelling:** Both returned to the kirtan; Maharaj sat near the neem tree (ovi 53). Govindbuva began with the exposition of one Bhagwat verse from the 11th skandha, from the Hansa-gita (ovi 54). He explained the first half in detail and Samarth spoke the second half; Govindbuva was bewildered in mind (ovi 55). He judged: this speaker of the second half seems truly authorised; go bring him to the temple (ovi 56). Bankatlal, Pitambar and others went with great humility (ovi 57-58) but Maharaj did not move at all (ovi 58). Govindbuva came himself, folded his hands, said "you are Shankar himself, it is not right to sit outside; without the worthy the temple is empty" (ovi 59-60), and that his earlier merit had borne fruit (ovi 61-62). Samarth answered with tattva: Govinda, keep speech and action consistent; you just taught that Ishwar pervades everything and nothing is left inside or outside, so why this stubbornness (हट्ट) (ovi 63-64)? Whatever one teaches, one must practise; a sadhak must never play with word-quibbling (ovi 65). You tell a Bhagwat verse and act against it; this is not the way of a kathekari (ovi 66). "Do not be a belly-filling kathekari on earth"; finish the kirtan, I will hear it from here (ovi 67). Govindbuva returned and announced to all: a priceless jewel has come to your Shegaon, look after it (ovi 68). This is no longer Shegaon, Pandharpur has been "spent" here, the walking, talking Pandurang has come (ovi 69). Keep a provision for him, serve him, obey his orders like Veda words (ovi 70); then your welfare is certain, do not lose this effortless treasure (ovi 71).
- **Moral/teaching:** practise what you preach (ovi 63-66); a saint cannot be summoned; even a famed kirtankar is humbled; the audience is told to treat Gajanan as Pandurang.

### Episode 6 - The invitation home and Manik Chowk

- **Ovi range:** 72-81.
- **Retelling:** The kirtan ended and people went home; Bankatlal reached home with joy that would not fit in his mind (ovi 72). He told his respected father with love, "Baba, bring Gajanan to our house" (ovi 73). Bhavaniram heard what his son said and told him with love "you yourself go and bring him" (ovi 74). With his father's consent Bankatlal was greatly pleased, wondering when he would meet the guru-murti and bring him home (ovi 75). Then "in Manik Chowk, on the fourth day" the sadgurunath met Bankatlal at sunset (ovi 76; timeline in section 5 q14). Dasganu: the sun of the day set, and the sun of knowledge rose at Manik Chowk to the east, through Bankatlal's fortune (ovi 77). Cowherds were bringing cows back to the village and cows gathered around Samarth (ovi 78); they felt Krishna (Nandasut) had come, and birds chirped in the trees (ovi 79). Lamps were being prepared and shopkeepers were busy when Bankat brought Maharaj home (ovi 80). The father saw the form and was overjoyed, prostrated at his feet and seated him on a "पाट" (ovi 81).
- **Moral/teaching:** devotion bears fruit when family consent and the saint's grace come together; animals respond to a saint.

### Episode 7 - Pradosh, the bilva leaves and the tray of puris

- **Ovi range:** 82-94.
- **Retelling:** The father folded his hands and asked him to eat: "you are Parvatikant (Shiva) himself and pradosh time has come" (ovi 82). He had heard in the Skandapurana that Shiva-worship at pradosh makes a person fortunate (ovi 83), so he brought bilva leaves at once and placed them on Samarth's head with deep devotion (ovi 84). He had told Gajanan "dine here", but cooking would take some time (ovi 85); if Gajanan did not wait, Parvatikant would leave the house fasting at pradosh (ovi 86). A huge crowd had gathered to watch (ovi 87). He thought: there are puris from noon in the house, put them on a tray and set it before Samarth (ovi 87-88). "They all know there is no deceit in my heart; I meet Umapati with feeling" (ovi 89); he is not giving "shile" food by choice, and for a cooked meal calling it stale would not be proper (ovi 90, section 5 q17). The tray was prepared at once as planned (ovi 91): puris, almonds, dates, bananas, mosambi and "मुळे" (ovi 92). Bukka was put on his forehead and a flower garland on his neck (ovi 92). The guru ate with a pleased mind, everything that fell on his plate, to the full (ovi 93). About three "sher" of food went into his stomach; Shri Gajanan Maharaj stayed there all night (ovi 94).
- **Moral/teaching:** hospitality offered as Shiva-worship; the saint accepts what is offered with an undeceiving heart.

### Episode 8 - Mangal-snan and the pitambar

- **Ovi range:** 95-103.
- **Retelling:** Next day Bankatlal gave Samarth a "मंगलस्नान" with great joy (ovi 95). About a hundred pots of hot water were poured by men and women "as the mind liked" (ovi 96). People rubbed shikekai and soap on his feet (ovi 97); others brought davana, hina, chameli oil and rubbed him with belia oil with their own hands (ovi 98); Bankatlal's house lacked nothing (ovi 99). After the bath a pitambar was put on him and he was seated with great honour "योगीराज गादीवरी" (ovi 100). Saffron-scented paste on his forehead, many garlands round his neck, some people poured tulsi-manjari on his head (ovi 101). Naivedya of many kinds was offered; Bankatlal's fortune rose (ovi 102). Dasganu says the house became Dwarka, and that day was Monday, Shiva's day (ovi 103).
- **Moral/teaching:** service with devotion makes a house a sacred place.

### Episode 9 - Ichcharam's naivedya and the vomiting

- **Ovi range:** 104-128 (embedded Ramdas comparison at ovi 122-124).
- **Retelling:** All the household had fulfilled their wishes except one, Ichcharam Shetji (ovi 104), Bankat's cousin, a devout Shankar bhakt (ovi 105), who thought: today is Monday, I am fasting, and Shankar has come to my house walking and talking (ovi 106). He meant to worship him at sunset and then break his fast (ovi 107). After sunset he bathed, mindful of pradosh time (ovi 108), took worship materials and worshipped "the sadhu Gajanan" with great love (ovi 109). He said: it is past noon, though you have eaten, eat something now (ovi 110); I will not eat until you eat, I have the Monday fast, Gurudeva (ovi 111); the wishes of all devotees were fulfilled, mine remains, please fulfil it (ovi 112). Curious people watched as Ichcharam brought a large plate (परात) of naivedya (ovi 113): ambemohar rice, "दोन मुदी" of rice, many pakwannas (ovi 114), jilebi, Raghavdas, motichur, karanji, anarase, ghee, many vegetable dishes (ovi 115), chutneys, koshimbir, a bowl of curd, a bowl of ghee (ovi 116), "food for four men" (ovi 117). Seeing it, Maharaj spoke to himself (आपणासी): "You say 'eating, eating day and night', Ganapya; now eat all this; Aghorya, do not dishonour it; all the people have come to see your aghor ways" (ovi 118-119). He sat to eat and finished all of it, leaving nothing in the plate, even the salt and lime (ovi 120). Dasganu: what is the end of insistence? This was shown; the guruvar made a kautuk (ovi 121). A "खणाणून" vomit came, the food coming back good (ovi 122). Comparison: Ramdas once longed for kheer, drank it to the throat, vomited, then ate it again to defeat the craving (ovi 122-124). Likewise Gajanan, to stop people's insistence quickly, did this vomiting, though he had strength in his body (ovi 125). A satpurush's conduct serves later generations and protects natural dharma (ovi 126). Samarth taught the people that insisting is not good and gives the opposite result (ovi 127). After the vomit the place was cleaned, he was seated as before and bathed again (ovi 128).
- **Moral/teaching:** do not insist on a saint; a saint masters appetite and desire (ovi 124-127); his acts are examples for later generations.

### Embedded comparison - Ramdas and the kheer (not counted)

- **Ovi range:** 122-124. Ramdas Swami, as a comparison only: a craving for kheer, he drank it to the throat, vomited, then ate it again to defeat desire. The source of this anecdote is not given here; any outside source would be "outside knowledge, not in this adhyay" and is not used.

### Episode 10 - Bhajan and the name "Gajanan"

- **Ovi range:** 129-134.
- **Retelling:** Men and women took darshan; Maharaj was in a joyful mood. Two "दिंड्या" came for bhajan (ovi 129). Their voices were sweet, the tone "खडे पहाडी", and they began Vitthal's name-chant with delight (ovi 130). Meanwhile Maharaj on his seat was muttering "गणगण गणांत बोतें" in the guise of bhajan (ovi 131). This was always his bhajan; people played "टिचक्या" and joy filled all night (ovi 132). Because "gan gan" went on always, people gave him the name "Gajanan" (ovi 133). Dasganu adds: for one who himself became Brahman, where do name and form come from; the confusion of names and forms rests on Prakriti (ovi 134).
- **Moral/teaching:** his name arises from his own muttering; a realised being has no real need of name and form.

**Counts:** 10 narrative episodes (+1 embedded anecdote not counted); 2 frame sections; 2 descriptive passages.

Called "योगेश्वर" (ovi 32, 43), "समर्थ" (ovi 5, 52), "सद्गुरुनाथ" (ovi 76), "गुरुमूर्ति" (ovi 75, 93), "महाराज" (ovi 56, 80), "साधु" (ovi 109), "स्वामी समर्थ गजानन" (ovi 138); calls himself "गणप्या" and "अघोऱ्या" (ovi 118-119). - **Body:** "अंगीं बळ असूनिया" (ovi 125), strong in body. Height, build, complexion, hair, beard, eyes: not mentioned. Appetite: about three sher in one meal (ovi 94), then a naivedya for four (ovi 117, 120).
- **Face:** not mentioned. Expression: laughs, "बोलले हांसोन" (ovi 49); "प्रसन्न चित्तें" (ovi 93); "आनंदवृत्ती" (ovi 129).
- **Clothing:** not mentioned before his bath. After the bath: "पितांबर तो नेसविला" (ovi 100), a pitambar (yellow silk cloth, inferred meaning of the word). - **Objects carried:** a gourd (तुंबा, ovi 33-44) which Pitambar fills; a chilim (ovi 146). Whether this तुंबा is the same vessel as the भोपळा of Adhyay 1 is not stated here (inferred similar).

- **Voice/speech style:** direct commands ("आण झुणका भाकरी", ovi 31; "काढ सुपारी खिशातून", ovi 46); teasing and sharp ("पोटभऱ्या कथेकरी", ovi 67); laughs when refusing money (ovi 49); lectures Govindbuva (ovi 63-67); speaks to himself and calls himself Ganapya and Aghorya (ovi 118-119). Voice pitch/quality: not mentioned.
- **Diet:** jhunka-bhakri and pithla (ovi 31-32), supari (ovi 45-46), tray of puris, almonds, dates, bananas, mosambi (ovi 92-94), huge naivedya (ovi 114-120), clear water (ovi 42) and muddy water (ovi 144). Vomits the naivedya (ovi 122).
- **Residence:** in this adhyay he stays in Bankatlal's house (ovi 80-94, 103, 137-139); no earlier residence stated.
- **Occupation:** yogeshwar/sadguru; not a householder (inferred).
- **Relationships:** Bankatlal brings him home (ovi 80); Bhavaniram hosts him (ovi 81-94); Ichcharam worships him (ovi 109); Govindbuva, Pitambar and Ramajipant relate to him as devotees or by report.

## 9. Continuity

- **Gajanan's appearance:** age not mentioned. Strength of body stated once (ovi 125). Clothing is never described until after the bath (pitambar, ovi 100); before that nothing is said in this adhyay, so the "almost naked" state of Adhyay 1 is not confirmed or denied here. Complexion, hair, beard: not mentioned. The pitambar appears only from ovi 100; whether he keeps it afterward is not stated.
- **Props:** the gourd appears at ovi 33 (not stated how Pitambar has it or where Samarth got it, inferred as his, "तुंबा" as in Adhyay 1); the chilim is only mentioned in the closing (ovi 146).
- **Where Samarth is physically:** back of the kirtan area (ovi 27) -> by the neem tree (ovi 52-53) -> Manik Chowk (ovi 76) -> Bankatlal's house for the night (ovi 94) and the following day(s) (ovi 95-139). Whether he is at the neem tree throughout the kirtan, and whether Pitambar is with him at ovi 52-53, is not stated.
- **Relationships carried or established:** Bankatlal knows Gajanan from Adhyay 1 (ovi 5). Pitambar does not know him until told (ovi 26). Govindbuva does not know him until ovi 55. Bhavaniram learns from his son (ovi 73). Ramajipant learns from Bankatlal (ovi 17). Ichcharam is introduced only at ovi 104.
- **Knowledge changes:** Pitambar is first doubtful, then convinced (ovi 34-43). Govindbuva moves from confusion to open worship (ovi 55-62, 68-71). Bankatlal's longing changes to joy at ovi 72 and to fulfilment at ovi 76-81.
- **Name continuity:** the narrator already calls him "गजानन" at ovi 6, 36; Bankatlal does so at ovi 73; yet ovi 133 explains the name as given by people. See section 5, question 40.
- **Timeline:** four days of longing (ovi 21); the Manik Chowk meeting is on "the fourth day" (ovi 76, unclear); one night stay (ovi 94); mangal-snan on the next day, a Monday (ovi 95, 103); Ichcharam's worship that evening at pradosh (ovi 108); vomit and second bath after (ovi 122-128); bhajan through the night (ovi 132). Is the ovi 82-86 pradosh the same day as the Manik Chowk meeting? The text says the father worried that Parvatikant would leave "fasting" (ovi 86) at pradosh; whether this is a Sunday or a Monday evening is not stated. The second pradosh (ovi 108) is Monday (ovi 106).
- **Changes within the chapter:** Samarth's clothing (first pitambar at ovi 100); his place (temple area -> house); his state (hungry/unadorned -> garlanded, seated on a gadi); Bankatlal's mood (anxious -> joyful); the crowd (a kirtan audience -> countless visitors, ovi 137).
- **Mood continuity of the host:** Bhavaniram goes from worried father (ovi 11-14) to reverent host (ovi 81-94).

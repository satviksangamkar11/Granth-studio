## The story: "First Appearance in Shegaon and Recognition at Devidas's Feast" (ovi 70-144)

**Scene 1 (ovi 70-83, 2 shots). First appearance.** Shot 1: historical Shegaon, a small village great in fame (58-60). Shot 2: the young Gajanan seen there. The narrator: this great man's place and caste cannot be traced (70). On screen, the caption "1878" (the year only). His coming to Shegaon in his prime (74, 83) is shown, not narrated.

**Scene 2 (ovi 84-96, 8 shots). The leaf plates.** Devidas, a devout Brahmin householder, is preparing a Ritushanti feast for one of his sons. Used leaf plates have been thrown on the road in front of his house. A man sits there in a worn old bandi, otherwise bare, detached from worldly labels, name and status, with an unfired clay pipe he made himself and a gourd for water. His gaze rests on the tip of his nose, and he shines like the young morning sun. He searches the plates and eats the grains of leftover rice that fall in his sight, to make people understand "food is Brahman". *Narrator (94-96):* the Upanishad says "food is Brahman", and the common person does not grasp the meaning.

**Scene 3 (ovi 97-105, 6 shots). Two men watch.** Bankatlal Agarwal and Damodarpant Kulkarni see him and are amazed. His doings look like a madman's. They reason: if he wanted food he would ask, and a wise host never turns a beggar away. They decide to stand on the road and test his conduct. A true sadhu may behave like a madman; this man may be mad or full of knowledge. *Narrator (106-107, not filmed):* thousands pass without noticing; only the connoisseur picks the gem.

**Scene 4 (ovi 108-112, 5 shots). The question and the silence.** Bankatlal steps forward humbly and asks why he searches the plates: if he is hungry, they will arrange food. There is no answer. He only looks up at both of them, steady, radiant, absorbed in inner bliss. The two visitors bow to him in silence.

**Scene 5 (ovi 113-118, 7 shots). The plate at the door.** After a request whose speaker is unnamed, Devidas sets a plate of rich cooked dishes at the door before the Swami. He sits, mixes all the dishes together into one, and eats without liking or disliking any taste. *Narrator (116-117):* one satisfied by the nectar of Brahman does not crave jaggery-water or smack his lips; a sovereign emperor does not delight in being given an estate.

**Scene 6 (ovi 119-122, 6 shots). "We were wrong."** Bankatlal tells Damodarpant that calling him mad was surely their mistake. As Arjuna "went mad" for Subhadra, this man went mad for liberation, and there is no need to test him any more. Ovi 122 praises Shegaon and Hari's gift of desirelessness (the speaker is unclear).

**Scene 7 (ovi 123-133, 9 shots). Noon, and the empty gourd.** It is noon and the ground is hot; the birds have gone to the trees. He sits in the full sun, happy and fearless (the speaker of 123-124 is unclear). Bankatlal notes that he has eaten his fill but his gourd has no water. Damodarpant offers: if he wishes, this servant is ready to bring water. Gajanan laughs and answers: if you need it, bring water; one Brahman fills the world and there is no difference between you and us, yet worldly conduct must be followed; our bodies ate, so they need water. Bankatlal says their fortune is blessed. Damodarpant goes into Devidas's house to fetch water in a gadva.

**Scene 8 (ovi 134-137, 5 shots). The trough.** While Damodarpant is away, Gajanan goes to the cattle trough beside the well, drinks, and belches with satisfaction. Damodarpant returns with the gadva and cries out: "Oh! Samarth, do not drink that muddy water, it is fit for animals!" He offers sweet, clear, cool water scented with vetiver.

**Scene 9 (ovi 138-144, 11 shots). One Brahman, and the run.** Gajanan says: do not tell us worldly tales. All moving and unmoving things are pervaded by Brahman; clear or muddy water, pleasant or unpleasant smell, are its forms. The drinker too is not separate. God's unfathomable play can be understood in this human birth. You have abandoned that vision and entangled your mind in worldly dealings; always reflect on what the world arose from. Both men are deeply moved and ready to fall at his feet. Seeing their intent, Gajanan runs off like the wind, to no place in particular, and no one can stop him.

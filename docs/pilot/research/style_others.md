# Style study: 5 Instagram mythology reel creators (artwork only)

Method: 10 phone screenshots (1290x2796), each viewed once. Instagram UI ignored. "OBSERVED" = literally visible in a frame. "INFERRED" = deduced from comparing frames of the same scene or from common practice. Hex values are eyeballed from screenshots (JPEG/H.264 compressed), accurate to roughly +/-10 per channel.

Limits to keep in mind: a screenshot is one instant. Where I say something "moves", I am comparing two or three instants, so speed, easing and loop length are unknown unless stated.

## 1. mythology.for.you (Hindi captions, cartoon)
Files: 84515864 (hut/sages scene), 50bbf71f (horse tail scene). These are two DIFFERENT scenes, not near-duplicates, so no motion can be derived by differencing; motion below is inferred from the artwork construction.

### OBSERVED
- Style family: flat vector "corporate cartoon" (Kurzgesagt/Ted-Ed-ish), with a Western-cartoon face and proportions, Indian costume/props. Not folk art.
- Line: uniform dark-brown/near-black outline (~3-4 px at 923 px width, approx #2B1D14) on every character and prop, round joins. Backgrounds (trees, forest trunks) are outlined less or not at all.
- Palette scene A (daytime hut): teal-grey forest wall #7FA3A6 / #9DB7B7 with light-shaft rays (lighter diagonal bands, ~#B9CFCB at low alpha), olive-green foliage #3F7A3A / #2E6B3A, thatch gold #D9A441 with darker stripes #B8862E, sand ground #E4CF9B, orange robe #F08A1C, blue sari #3A4FB5, cream sari #F2E8D0, teal-green blouse #2E9E7B, skin tan #D9A47A.
- Palette scene B (sunset sea): vertical gradient lavender-purple #5E5A9E top -> peach #F4B27A -> warm orange horizon #F6A04D, then dusky mauve water #8E7A8C; horse pale pink-white #F2DDE0 with mustard saddle #F4B13E, blue dot #2F83C9. Thin cream horizontal "cloud" streaks #F8C98B and white-ish water dashes.
- Depth/layers: scene A has at least 5 layers: sky/backdrop with light rays, tree-trunk row, hut + banana tree + bush, ground, characters, big foreground leaf canopy cropped at top corners (frames the shot, dark green #2F6B3A with dark outline). Scene B: gradient sky, streaks, horizon sea, ripple ellipses (white thin rings around the horse), foreground dark polygon (boat or rock edge, brown-translucent with thick black outline) cutting in from the right = foreground parallax object.
- Lighting: soft light shafts in A; a gradient "golden hour" glow in B; no real shading on characters (flat fills with a few hard-edged secondary shadow shapes, e.g. cloth folds).
- Character construction: very rigid. Characters face camera, arms hanging straight, hands clasped; clearly built from separate cut-out shapes (head, torso, sari, arms, bangles) which suggests puppet rigging in After Effects/Moho-style. Faces are simple: large eyes with thick lashes, bindi, small smile. Long vertical fold lines on the white sari.
- Small particles: tiny white blobs in the ground (the oval white "dots" arranged in an arc on the sand look like a decorative path or floating motes), small 4-point sparkles and tiny birds ("v" shapes) in the sky in B.
- Caption: Hindi Devanagari, heavy rounded bold display weight (Mukta/Khand/Poppins-Devanagari Black style), fill yellow #FFD21F, dark brown outline ~4-5 px (#3A2510), plus a soft offset drop shadow (down-right ~4 px, ~40% black). Placed centered, lower third (about 70% down the frame, y~1390/2000), size about 90-100 px cap height (very large for a phone). Only 2-3 words visible ("की है।", "की पूँछ में,"), so it is PHRASE-CHUNK / few-words-at-a-time, not whole sentences. The text sits on top of the art with no box.
- Border/frame: none. Full-bleed.

### INFERRED
- Motion: probably subtle idle animation on characters (breathing, blink, slight head tilt), slow camera push-in or pan over a flat painted background (the cropped foreground leaves in A suggest parallax: foreground leaves drift faster than trunks). In B the foreground dark polygon sliding across and the horse in the left third suggest a horizontal pan with parallax. Water streaks and ripple rings probably scroll/scale-fade in a loop.
- Caption animation: each chunk pops/scales in (typical "pop" with overshoot) and is replaced, in sync with voice-over.
- Number of animated things per shot: low (3-6): camera, 1-2 character idles, ripples, light rays, 1-2 birds/sparkles.

- A white paper-plane/arrow-shaped small white triangle near x=320,y=860 (between tree and heads) is in all 3 frames but its position moves slightly: appears to be a flying small object (likely an arrow or a spear tip, or a floating particle).
- Particles: Different positions in each frame, i.e. they DRIFT. Examples: bright mint-white dot at (557,635) in c286e2df, (195,770) in f0cb403c, (460,605) in f530e202; dots near the grass at right (800,1450), (610,1430) move. I count roughly 10-20 drifting orbs, some seem to move more than 100 px between frames, so they float in loops; a few are also scaled (large soft blobs at (810,1465) in f530e202, "bokeh"). Looks like fireflies drifting upward/sideways with fading.
- Grass: static.
- Number of animated things: ~10 arms + sword + head row bob + Sita's arm + ~15-20 particles + caption swap. Mostly small-amplitude rotational motion with a lot of "life" from particles.
- Caption between frames: caption replaced as a whole line (sentence-level), with a tiny scale/position difference. No highlight/word-by-word.
- Camera: no pan or zoom evident (moon, trees, border all at same coordinates across all 3 frames). The frame is locked off; all life comes from character-part motion and particles.

### INFERRED

- The reel is a story told in this tableau style, with the single locked shot lasting several seconds.

# Cross-creator analysis

## (a) Common techniques that make reels look alive and "full" with modest animation
1. A fixed shell around a mostly static drawing: the frame/border (Madhubani triangles, folk dotted frame) never moves, which makes the whole thing feel designed and "complete" (devanshu, the.indian.tales0).
2. Constant ambient particles: fireflies/orbs (devanshu, ~15-20 dots), drifting petals/sprigs (the.indian.tales0, ~12-15 ornaments with ~10-25 px drift between frames), snow flecks and stars (mythyatra, first100stories), sparkles and birds (mythology.for.you). These fill empty space and give constant motion at nearly zero animation cost.
3. Hinged cut-out idle loops: Ravana's 10 arms and sword (rotating 20-45 degrees), Sita's 2-segment arm, Shiva's hair and trident sway. The body stays rigid. A few rotating limbs on a locked-off camera reads as "alive".
4. One "hero" moving element on a looping path: fish and a flowing wave pattern along the Ganga ribbon; water streaks/ripple rings in the lake; clouds puffing at the hair. Scrolling a pattern along a path is a repeating texture, cheap.
5. Layered parallax with a cropped foreground object (mythology.for.you canopy leaves and dark foreground polygon; first100stories reed strip; mythyatra figures in front of mountains). Even a 2-5 percent camera move then sells 3D.
6. Atmosphere over lines: light shafts, fog bands, glow/rim light, gradient skies (first100stories, mythology.for.you, mythyatra). These make flat shapes read as lit spaces.
7. Colour discipline: 5-8 flat colours with one strong complementary accent (navy+gold, red+blue-grey, aubergine+gold, violet+red eyes).
8. Strong caption layer carrying the rhythm: large phrase pops (mythology.for.you yellow + outline), keyword highlight in gold (first100stories), whole sentence at the top (devanshu), serif word-by-word fades (the.indian.tales0), parchment plate (mythyatra). Each has a single consistent template across scenes.
9. Scene transitions that mask the cheapness: dissolve/blur (first100stories 33da3e76 shows macroblocks = fast transition), hair unfurl or ribbon shifting as a beat in the story (the.indian.tales0).
10. Wide readable silhouettes. Faces are tiny or absent (back view, silhouette, side profile, mask-like), so no lip sync or detailed facial animation is needed (first100stories back view, mythyatra silhouettes, Madhubani flat profile). Voice-over carries the narrative.

## (b) Cheapest-to-produce techniques with high visual payoff (ranked)
1. Particle overlay (drifting dots/petals/embers) - one looped alpha video reused for every episode; payoff high, cost near zero.
2. Slow Ken-Burns / push-in 3-8% on a locked composition with parallax between 3-5 layers (Blender 2.5D or ffmpeg zoompan).
3. Silhouette characters + rim-light + gradient sky (mythyatra): no face art needed, consistent between episodes.
4. Repeating decorative frame + caption template (devanshu / the.indian.tales0).
5. Pivot rotation on 2-4 limbs / hair / cloth with ease-in-out sin motion (±3-10 deg, 2-4 s period) - few keyframes, mostly sine drivers.
6. Scrolling pattern on a ribbon/river (UV scroll), looped fish/boat along a path.
7. Animated ripples (scaling thin ellipses fading out) and water streak lines sliding horizontally.
8. Glow pulses on a lamp / eyes / sun (opacity oscillation 0.7-1.0).
9. Word-chunk captions with pop and keyword colour highlight (ffmpeg drawtext/ASS).
10. Dissolve/blur transitions between stills, and shot reuse (same background layer, new character pose).

## (c) Which style suits a 21-episode Marathi/Hindi devotional series about Gajanan Maharaj (Shegaon), recurring characters
Recommendation: a hybrid of "flat folk/poster vector with simple line + paper texture + decorative frame" (devanshu / the.indian.tales0 family) for characters and props, lit with the soft rim-light / gradient-sky atmosphere of first100stories, with mythyatra-style near-silhouette used for crowds, minor figures and night scenes.

Why:
- Recurring characters: a rigid flat cut-out with a fixed design (Maharaj: bare-chested, dhoti, a bhagwa/saffron or white cloth, bearded/unkempt hair, a chillim/pipe, Bankatlal, Damodar, the villagers) can be generated ONCE as separate parts and reused across all 21 episodes; consistency is the main failure mode of image models, and flat style with strong outlines hides drift. Painterly style (first100stories) makes the same face look different each time.
- Devotional respect: a saint who was a real historical figure (1878-ish, Shegaon) should not be a cartoon with comic proportions (mythology.for.you style would risk a jokey tone). Folk-art flat style with a calm stance and frontal/back/profile poses feels reverent and traditional (Madhubani/Warli/Pattachitra feeling is already read as devotional). Rim-lit back views/silhouettes allow depicting the saint without needing a "likeness" of the face.
- Marathi context: rich local identity is possible via a regional motif frame (e.g. Warli stick figures/Paithani border/Maharashtrian temple (Shegaon Sansthan) lamp-stambh motifs, bhagwa flag) the way devanshu uses Madhubani and the.indian.tales0 uses a folk frame; template reusable for 21 episodes.
- Production cost: flat shapes + limited palette => AI image models give clean separated layers, 2.5D renderer parallax works well, captions can be large and templated; Devanagari captions in a big bold font are legible (mythology.for.you style).
- Typography: Devanagari bold (Mukta/Khand/Hind/Baloo/Tiro Devanagari Marathi) at ~85-100 px for 1080 wide, phrase of 2-4 words at a time, accent colour on keyword, soft shadow. Avoid placing it high (devanshu collides with Instagram UI) - put it in the lower third at y~60-68% of height, inside the 1080x1920 safe zone (keep ~150 px clear at the top, ~320 px at the bottom, ~120 px at the right).

## (d) Concrete per-shot recipe (flat images from an image model + 2.5D layered renderer in Blender + ffmpeg)
Target: 1080x1920, 30 fps, shot length 3-6 s (150-180 frames at 30 fps at most), 21 episodes x ~12-20 shots.

### Layers to generate per shot (as separate flat images, all with transparent background except layer 1)
Generate each on a plain flat colour (e.g. #00FF00 or white) then matte/rembg, or generate with "isolated on solid background" prompts. Same style prefix in every prompt (style bible: "flat vector folk illustration, thick black outline 4px, limited palette X, paper texture, no gradients, no text") plus a fixed seed or a reference image for each recurring character.
1. L0 SKY/BACKDROP: gradient or flat colour plus a few stars/clouds/moon. 1080x1920 (render at 1.25x = 1350x2400 if you plan a push-in, so you do not upscale).
2. L1 FAR: mountains / temple skyline / hills (alpha). Place far, scale 1.0.
3. L2 MID: trees/hut/temple/ground midground (alpha).
4. L3 GROUND/WATER: ground band or river (opaque band, alpha top).
5. L4 CHARACTER BODY PARTS (alpha): for each recurring character, separate PNGs: head (with face), torso, upper arm L/R, forearm+hand L/R, legs/dhoti, hair/beard, cloth (shawl/ends), optional prop (staff/pipe). Generate them once per character, per pose-view (front, 3/4, back, profile) and store in a character library; reuse in all 21 episodes. Keep parts with a ~10% overlap at the joints so rotation does not show gaps (devanshu/the.indian.tales0 parts show clear gaps and still read fine, so this is forgiving).
6. L5 FOREGROUND FRAME: a cropped leaf/branch/pillar/reed strip (alpha) for foreground parallax and vignette (mythology.for.you canopy).
7. L6 FX (generated once, reused): looped particle clip (see below), light-shaft PNG, fog band PNG, glow sprite (radial gradient), ripple ring PNG, water streak PNG, petal/leaf sprites (6-8 variants), smoke/cloud puff sprites.
8. FRAME overlay (generated once for the series): Warli/Paithani/temple-border PNG 1080x1920 with transparent centre, thickness about 40-60 px (devanshu ~60 px at 923 width ≈ 70 px at 1080).
9. Caption: not an image, done in ffmpeg (see below).

### Blender 2.5D setup (Blender 5.2 at D:/tools/blender)
- Each layer = a plane with an emission-shaded (or Principled with emission 1.0) image-texture material, alpha mode "channel packed", Blend mode Alpha Hashed/Blend, Interpolation Linear (or Smart), "Extension Clip". Use an orthographic or a perspective camera with focal length 50 mm. Camera 1080x1920, Eevee (not Cycles) for speed; film Filter 1.0 px, turn off Bloom unless you need it; color management "Standard", not Filmic/AgX (otherwise the flat colours go dull).
- Z spacing (metres) from camera: L0 sky 40, L1 far 28, L2 mid 18, L3 ground 12, L4 characters 8, L5 foreground 3. Scale each plane so it exactly fills the frame at its distance (plane width = distance * 2*tan(fov/2)*aspect) so the parallax comes out of the real perspective.
- Camera move (Ken Burns + parallax): dolly in by 6-10% of the distance to the character layer over the whole shot (e.g. Z from 8.0 m to 7.3 m... practical: camera z 0 -> -0.6 m toward the scene) plus a lateral drift of 0.3-0.5 m (about 2-4% of the frame width at the character layer, which makes the far layers shift less) in the opposite or same direction; Bezier ease in/out (Blender default) or linear for slow moves; Choose 1 move type per shot (push-in / pull-back / pan left / pan right / tilt up). First100stories pull-back range was ~1.7x in a transition, so allow up to 1.7x only in dramatic reveals.
- Character idle (sine drivers, no keyframes): arm rotation ±4-8 degrees (period 3-4 s, different phase per arm), head rotation ±2 degrees (period 4-6 s), torso Y scale 1.0 to 1.015 (breath, period 3.5 s), cloth/hair ends ±6-10 degrees with 0.3 s lag (period 2.5 s). Use driver expression like `0.1*sin(frame/fps*2*pi/T + phase)` (radians). Origins at joints (shoulder/elbow/neck).
- Limb gesture beat (one per shot at the story moment): forearm 0 -> 45 degrees over 12-18 frames with ease-out, hold, return over 24 frames. Ravana arm swings in devanshu are 20-45 degrees. For hands raised in blessing: raise the arm 60 degrees.
- Water: UV scroll of a streak texture on the water plane, X speed 0.02-0.04 UV/s; ripple rings = ring PNG planes that scale 0.2 -> 1.0 while alpha 0.8 -> 0 over 2.0 s, 2-3 staggered; ring ellipse ratio 3:1 for lake surfaces.
- River/ribbon (Ganga-like) path: a curve + follow-path constraint for fish/boats (speed ~ 1 path length per 3-4 s, looping), wave-glyph texture scroll 0.15-0.3 UV/s along the ribbon.
- Light: a glow sprite (additive: Blend "Add" or Emission shader + Transparent mix) behind/around the lamp, sun, halo: opacity oscillation 0.75-1.0, period 2-3 s, scale 1.0-1.05. Light shafts: 2-3 translucent diagonal planes, alpha 0.12-0.2, slow lateral drift ±10 px (period ~8 s), additive.
- Depth of field: OFF or very mild (devanshu/the.indian.tales0 have none). If painterly: only a fog plane (alpha 0.25-0.35) at the horizon, lighter than the sky, between L1 and L2.
- Render to PNG sequence (RGB, no alpha) at 1080x1920 (or 540x960 as a test); 4-8 s per shot; use Eevee samples 16 (flat colours need no more), Output "PNG, 8-bit, compression 15". Keep audio out of Blender.

### FX particles (ffmpeg or pre-rendered clip) - numeric suggestions
- Fireflies/orbs (night scenes, devanshu style): 18-24 particles, diameters 6-22 px at 1080 px width, 30% soft (Gaussian blur radius 4-6 px) and 70% hard, colours white #FFFFFF, mint #CFF7E0, pale yellow #FFF3A8, a few small ringed gold #F0C040; motion: drift 20-60 px/s, direction mostly up/right, with sine wobble ±15 px (period 3-5 s), fade in/out 0.5 s, lifetime 3-6 s, loop 8 s total, blend "screen" or normal alpha 0.6-1.0.
- Falling petals/leaves (day scenes, the.indian.tales0 style): 12-16 sprites 30-60 px, drift down 10-25 px/s (very slow), sideways wobble ±20 px, rotation ±40 degrees over 6-8 s, alpha 0.9. Colours: cream flower #F4EBD0 and green sprig #4A7A3A with white berries.
- Snow/embers (mythyatra style): 30-50 flecks 2-6 px white #FFFFFF alpha 0.5-0.9, speed 15-40 px/s diagonal; embers #FFB347 rising 40-80 px/s with flicker.
- Implementation options: (1) Blender: a Particle System (emit from a plane, "Billboard" instance of a sprite) or a Geometry Nodes scatter with an animated offset, rendered once as an alpha PNG sequence (or ProRes 4444/WebM VP9 alpha), 8 s loop, 1080x1920, then overlay with ffmpeg; reuse across ALL shots with different loop offsets. (2) Fully in ffmpeg without a render: use `geq`/`noise` hacks - not recommended; better to do (1) or a Python script with PIL that draws sprites to frames piped into ffmpeg (`-f image2pipe`).
- ffmpeg overlay example: `ffmpeg -i shot.mp4 -stream_loop -1 -i particles.webm -filter_complex "[0][1]overlay=0:0:shortest=1:format=auto" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p out.mp4`.

### Captions (ffmpeg ASS recommended over drawtext for per-word styling)
- Use libass: generate an .ass file from a transcript with word timestamps (e.g. from a forced aligner or just estimated from the voice-over). Burn-in: `-vf "ass=captions.ass"` (fonts via `fontsdir=`). Style: Devanagari font (Mukta ExtraBold / Baloo 2 ExtraBold / Hind SemiBold / Noto Sans Devanagari Black), FontSize 84-96 (on a 1080x1920 PlayRes), PrimaryColour yellow #FFD21F (or warm white #F4EDE0), OutlineColour dark brown #3A2510, Outline 5-6, Shadow 3-4 (offset), Bold=1, Alignment 2 (bottom-centre) with MarginV 560-620 px (i.e. about 68-70% down the frame, above the Instagram caption/UI zone), MarginL/MarginR 120. Max 2-4 words per event, 0.35-0.8 s each, with a pop effect via `{\fscx60\fscy60\t(0,120,\fscx105\fscy105)\t(120,200,\fscx100\fscy100)}` and a `{\fad(40,60)}`. Highlight keyword in gold #E8B84A with `{\c&H4AB8E8&}word{\r}` (ASS uses BGR order).
- Marathi: set `\fn` to a font that has Marathi conjuncts shaping properly (libass with HarfBuzz handles Devanagari; test "ज्ञ", "श्री", "क्ष", "गजानन महाराज"). Keep any English or Hinglish out unless needed.
- Style option "parchment plate" (mythyatra): render a deckled-edge PNG box (#EDE5C8, ~700x130 px) once, overlay it at y~230 px with fade in/out 0.2 s; place the ASS text (serif, colour #2A1E18, size 56) inside it. Good for chapter-title cards and quotes (for verses or ovis).
- Safe zone: keep text between x=120..960 and y=250..1500 (avoid the top 180 px and right-hand 130 px UI column, and the bottom 360 px).

### Colour grade and finishing (ffmpeg)
- Paper grain: overlay a seamless grain PNG (alpha 0.06-0.10, blend "overlay"/"softlight") `-filter_complex "[0][1]blend=all_mode=overlay:all_opacity=0.08"`; or `noise=alls=6:allf=t` (light temporal noise) for a film feel.
- Vignette: `vignette=PI/5` (mild), or use a premade vignette PNG with alpha 0.25 (darkens the top/bottom bands like first100stories).
- Slight sharpen/colour: `eq=saturation=1.08:contrast=1.04`, `unsharp=5:5:0.5` (flat colours do not need much).
- Overlay the decorative frame PNG last (before captions or after, as you prefer; captions after so they stay above everything).
- Shot-to-shot transitions: `xfade=transition=fade:duration=0.4:offset=...` for dissolves (a 0.4-0.5 s dissolve is what first100stories seems to use), `xfade=transition=smoothleft` / `wipeleft` for scene changes between places, a quick 6-frame `gblur` ramp (sigma 0 -> 12 -> 0) + dissolve for "time passes/vision" beats. Hard cuts for fast beats.
- Encode: `-c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 17 -pix_fmt yuv420p -r 30 -movflags +faststart` and AAC 192k, because Instagram will recompress (the macroblocks in first100stories 33da3e76 show what happens at too low a bitrate in transitions: encode at a high bitrate, CRF 15-17, then let Instagram recompress).

### Per-shot checklist (template)
1. Pick shot type: (A) establishing wide (sky+far+mid+ground, no character, push-in 6-8%, particles), (B) character medium (back/side view, idle sine, push-in 4-6%, particles, light glow), (C) silhouette group (mythyatra style: one flat dark plum #1E1424 plane per figure with a 4-6 px warm rim glow, crowd 3-6 figures, slow dolly + glow pulse on lamps), (D) detail/insert (hand with prop, lamp, flower, feet at a temple step: scale 1.0 -> 1.06, rack-fade), (E) title card (frame + parchment plate + big Devanagari, no motion except grain/particles).
2. Generate layers L0-L6 (L4 from the character library, L0-L3 and L5 per shot; ~4 new generated images per shot, ~60-80 per episode, ~1,500 in total for 21 episodes unless you reuse backgrounds (you can: e.g. 8 recurring locations: Shegaon village lane, the pipal tree, the temple/mutt, the well, a river bank (Purna), the jungle, a night scene with a lamp, and a gathering hall); with reuse you only need to generate ~8 backdrops x 3 times of day (dawn, day, dusk) x 3 layer = ~72 images in total plus character parts.
3. Blender: import layers via the "Import Images as Planes" add-on (shader Emit, blend Blend) then set Z spacing, the camera move, the sine drivers; render 3-6 s PNG sequence.
4. ffmpeg: image sequence -> mp4 (`-framerate 30 -i %04d.png`), overlay particles (+ light shafts if not in Blender), grain, vignette, frame, then captions (ASS), then concat shots with xfade, mix voice-over + music (sidechain duck: `sidechaincompress` on the music by the voice).
5. QA: view on a phone-size preview (540x960) and check: caption legibility at thumbnail size, nothing important in the unsafe zones, character tone respectful (no cartoon gags for the saint), and the continuity of colours for each recurring character.

### Style bible summary for the series (suggested defaults)
- Outline: black #1B1410, 4 px at 1080 width on characters/props, none on backgrounds (backgrounds use value contrast) or 2 px; Madhubani-style dashed inner line only if you go full folk look.
- Palette per episode: 6-8 colours: a deep background colour (indigo #1D2150 night / terracotta #B8401A day / dusk mauve #5A4D80), saffron #F0A020, white-cream #F0E6CC, green #3F7A3A, accent red #C8282A, accent gold #E8B84A, skin tones for Maharaj (warm brown #B97C54).
- Frame: a Maharashtrian motif border (e.g. Warli triangles/figures or a Paithani peacock-lotus strip) 60-70 px, constant across episodes.
- Caption: Devanagari bold 90 px, yellow fill/brown outline/soft shadow, keyword in saffron or gold, lower-third at y~68%.
- Motion budget per shot: 1 camera move + 1-3 limb/hair/cloth loops + 1 environmental loop (water/leaves/smoke) + 1 particle layer + 1 glow. Don't exceed ~25 moving elements (the.indian.tales0 manages ~25 and still looks rich).
